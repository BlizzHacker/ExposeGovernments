Funding the Truth
Where Nobody Is Watching

The Move Weight Foundation runs 14 public records chapters across 3 states — 4 in Oklahoma, 7 in Texas, 3 in Mississippi. We archive the meetings, file open records requests in our own name so residents stay anonymous, and publish every document that comes back, whole and unredacted. It is the same job in a town of thirteen thousand and in the largest city in Texas: somebody has to read the agenda. Every dollar files another request.

Three chapters are written up in depth below because they have the longest record behind them. They are not the whole Foundation — there are fourteen, and the full list is further down. Each applies the same playbook to a different place: file the records, publish the documents, keep a dated and sourced timeline. The playbook travels. The local facts do not.

San Angelo & Tom Green County, Texas — Est. 2026

San Angelo sold 343–374 acres of city-owned land for a 1.2‑gigawatt data center campus, wrote three ordinances to govern it in five weeks, and never commissioned an independent impact study — the City says so itself. Three council members have faced recall petitions over it. The San Angelo chapter keeps the dated record, files Texas Public Information Act requests, and publishes every document that comes back.

Give Time Instead

The work is done by people, and we count their hours

14 chapters and 5,423 indexed meetings are almost entirely volunteer work. There are two jobs and both are real. Local foot workers go to the meeting, photograph the posted agenda, walk the ward and send back what they saw — nothing about that requires a computer. Developers keep the portal adapters alive, because a city can change its agenda system on a Tuesday and take a chapter dark until somebody notices.

Hours logged in the volunteer register are what a grant application asks for and what the Form 990 narrative rests on. There is no password — you put in your email, we send you a link.

Where we work

14 chapters. Three states down, forty-seven to go.

Every state has a public records law. Almost nowhere does anyone use it on the ordinary business of local government — the consent agenda, the utility board packet, the tax abatement that passes in nine seconds. The Foundation builds a chapter, files the requests in its own name so residents stay anonymous, and publishes every document that comes back, in full and permanently.

Every Dollar Files

FOIA requests cost money. Printing fees, postage, certified mail — the city charges for every page. One comprehensive request can cost $25–$150 in government fees alone.

Your donation goes directly to funding public records requests across all fourteen chapters — whichever city the next document has to be prised out of. We publish every funded request and every government response. 100% transparency on where your money goes.

  • ✓ $25 — Funds one targeted FOIA request (printing + postage)
  • ✓ $50 — Funds a comprehensive records pull on a city department
  • ✓ $100 — Funds a full FOIA blitz on multiple targets
  • ✓ $250+ — Sustained funding for ongoing surveillance & records campaigns
  • ✓ Any amount — Pooled into the FOIA Fund and allocated to the highest-priority requests first

Our Mission

Sunlight Is the Best
Disinfectant

The Move Weight Foundation exists for one reason: to ensure that government operates in the open. We sponsor technology, fund records requests, and build tools that make transparency unavoidable.

When the city of Miami, Oklahoma threatened a citizen with prosecution for complaining about a dog attack — we built a transparency platform. When they stopped accepting FOIA requests by email — we built a proxy system. When they deleted Facebook content — we archived it. Every obstacle they create, we route around.
— Move Weight Foundation
