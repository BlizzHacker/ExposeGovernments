THE MIAMI DOWNTOWN REDEVELOPMENT AUTHORITY (MDRA) MET IN REGULAR MEETING MONDAY, JANUARY 20, 2026, IN THE MIAMI CIVIC CENTER COUNCIL CHAMBERS AT 4:00 PM WITH THE FOLLOWING MEMBERS PRESENT:

Lynn Forkum, Chairman Kyla Jones, Trustee Brian Estep, Trustee Cori Stotts, Trustee Joe Morgan, Trustee Bo Reese, Trustee (Absent) Jordan Boyd, Trustee (Absent)

The agenda was filed in the office of the City Clerk and posted in the main hallway and the north outside entryway of the Civic Center at 9:30 AM on November 14, 2025

THE TRUST MAY DISCUSS, CONSIDER, AND VOTE ON ANY ITEM LISTED IN THIS AGENDA:

Call to Order Chairman Forkum called the meeting to order at 4:00 PM

Public Input and Unscheduled Personal Appearances None

CONSENT AGENDA By unanimous consent the public body may designate noncontroversial items to be considered in one motion and one vote. The public body may add items from the regular agenda and approve. Posted agenda items not added to the consent docket will be considered separately in their regular order. Staff recommends that Items 4 and 5 be placed on the consent agenda.

Estep: Aye, Stotts: Aye, Morgan: Aye, Jones: Abstain, Chairman Forkum: Aye, Boyd: Absent, Reese: Absent

Approve Claims: Jan 2026 Moved to the consent agenda.

Financials: There was no representative from Finance. The Board moved on to the next topic.

None Coleman Storefront Windows Gretchen Frazier, Owner of Flint Creek Bath made an appeal to the board concerning the front windows of the Coleman Shops. She understands that repairs are needing to happen. She requests the tile underneath be repaired and every step taken to keep the aluminum trim of the windows to keep the stores matching the look of the Coleman Theatre. none Building Repair Project: The Chairman immediately gave the floor to Kevin Browning to speak on the Coleman Building repair. He doesn’t disagree with keeping the look. The tile is breaking due to weathering and rot and definitely needs to be replaced. There is a bid to replace the windows and go with a thicker pane that would not be as easy to break. He wants to be sure the board is thinking what will happen if there is damage. For many years there were many band aids put on issues at the Coleman and there are many projects that will need to be updated, such as the fire escape, the boilers, heat and Air.. Some current cost estimates would be $34,000 to rebuild the bottom with the tile. Another $34,000 for the doors and windows, and finally $9000 for the upper windows. Some other projects that need to be done are the boilers, Central Heat and Air being replaced next week. The city has a major problem with the fire escape in its current state.

Gretchen had a contact that could do the windows and keep the trim. The new goals are to get a quote on the fire escape. Get numbers to bring next month. The concerns are the liability from fragile windows, and the money from MDRA being used effectively for the city building. There are currently three bids for the windows from Oklahoma Glass, Joplin Glass, and Draft Brothers. Other Coleman Shop owners were asked their opinion. Carole from the Friends of the Coleman hopes the copper can be kept. Jason remembered a cap made to help with missing copper. Suggests that chatgpt could help with finding tile businesses. Wants the new windows to last, but anything that can be saved would be preferred.

None Coleman Theatre Managing Director Report Danny Dillon gave the Coleman Director’s report. The winter is the slower season, but the Coleman has lots of tours upcoming. There are shows upcoming including a comedy show January 22, The Whitney Houston Experience January 31, and an illusionist wanting to do 50 states in 50 days wants to come to the Coleman. A Selena tribute on February 20th. Lots of shows upcoming.

None Main Street Update: Jesse Black gave an update on Main Street. The New Years Eve Ball Drop was held for the first time and was a huge success. PlacerAi registered 848 people and that only counts cell phones, so the numbers are estimated closer to 1000. Main Street held two workshops January 5 and 6. Marketing and Window Display. Those were requested by the Main Street Committees. There is a Sip and Shop coming up on February 10, and a new shopping event, Where’s Bless O’Parker the week of St. Patrick’s Day.

None Other New Business, if any, which has Arisen since the Posting of the Agenda and Could Not Have been Anticipated Prior to the Time of Posting (25 O.S. §311 (9)) none. Trustee Community Announcements: none Adjournment Trustee Stotts made a motion to adjourn. Trustee Jones the second. The trust was polled with the following results:

Stotts: Aye, Jones: Aye, Estep: Aye, Morgan: Aye, Chairman Forkum: Aye, Boyd: Absent, Reese: Absent

Chairman Forkum declared the motion carried. Adjourned at 4:41 P.M.

Building Maintenance Update and Possible Vote on Storefront Window Project:

Kevin Browning presented an update on the building. For the windows Browning gave a quote on cost of repairing as is, or building brand new. The differences being quoted $36,000, or $53,500 from two different quotes for as is, as opposed to $77,000 for building new. The benefits of repairing as is would allow more funds to be used elsewhere on repairs in the Coleman. The benefits of brand new would be knowing better what we get and sealing everything up. There are some contractors that could do it as is. They believe they can do it. Either way it would need to have a quick timeframe to get each window and not disrupt business. The windows have leaked water before, and it could again. Tempered glass would take at least a couple of weeks to get here. There would be a warranty for either. The main goal would be making it last. Trustee Morgan asked about other projects that could use the extra funds if the trust went with going as is. Browning mentioned the fire escape as brought up last meeting would need $48,000 to $52,500. There is always a place where the money can go. The trust discussed the benefit of being able to tackle more projects.

Trustee Estep moved to approve fixing the window as is instead of brand new. Trustee Stotts the second. The trust was polled with the following results:

Estep: Aye, Stotts: Aye, Morgan: Aye, Jones: Abstain, Chairman Forkum: Aye, Boyd: Absent, Reese: Absent Chairman Forkum declared the motion carried. ,

Main Street Update:

Black also gave a report that the Federal Reserve of Kansas City was releasing a publication on downtown communities on Route 66. Jesse, Tori and Eli Chenoweth, Jordan Boyd, and Colby Allen were all interviewed for it.
