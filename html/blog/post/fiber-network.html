Miami High Speed Fiber: Promises vs. Reality

May 29, 2026 | Deep Dive

The Miami Sewer and Utilities Authority (MSUA) has officially launched the Miami High Speed Fiber Network project. On the surface, this sounds like great news for the community. But infrastructure projects of this scale demand scrutiny.

The Big Picture: Fiber optic infrastructure is a multi-million dollar investment that will shape Miami's digital future for decades. Who gets the contracts? Who profits? And will every neighborhood actually get connected?

What's Been Announced

MSUA has launched a high-speed fiber network project intended to bring gigabit internet capability to Miami, Oklahoma. This type of infrastructure can transform a community — enabling remote work, better education access, telemedicine, and economic development.

The promise is compelling. The questions are critical.

Questions That Need Answers

  1. Who was awarded the construction contracts? Were they competitively bid? Were local contractors considered? Were there connections between contractors and decision-makers?
  2. What's the total project cost? Initial announcements rarely include the full price tag. Cost overruns on infrastructure projects are common — who absorbs them?
  3. What's the deployment timeline? Fiber projects often promise quick delivery but face years of delays. What are the milestones and what happens if they're missed?
  4. Will all neighborhoods be served equally? Or will wealthy areas get fiber first while underserved communities wait? This pattern is common in municipal broadband projects nationwide.
  5. What will it cost consumers? The infrastructure is only valuable if people can afford to use it. What are the projected rates? Will there be subsidized tiers for low-income households?
  6. Who manages the network long-term? Operations and maintenance costs don't disappear after construction. What's the 10-year financial plan?

Why This Matters

When a public authority invests millions in infrastructure, every citizen has a stake in ensuring the money is spent wisely. Fiber networks are not just utility projects — they're economic development engines that can determine a community's competitiveness for years to come.

"Show me the budget and I'll show you the priorities. Show me the contracts and I'll show you the connections."

Miami has the opportunity to get this right. But getting it right requires transparency at every stage — from procurement through deployment to ongoing operations.

What We're Doing

We've filed requests for public records related to the fiber project, including:

  • All contracts and procurement documents
  • Project cost estimates and financial projections
  • Deployment maps and service area plans
  • Meeting minutes where the project was discussed and approved

We'll publish what we find. If you have insider knowledge about this project — whether you're a city employee, contractor, or concerned citizen — we want to hear from you. tips@exposemiamiok.com. Your identity stays protected.
