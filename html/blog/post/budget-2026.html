2026/2027 Budget: Where Is the Money Really Going?

May 29, 2026 | Analysis

The City of Miami recently posted a Public Hearing Notice for the 2026/2027 Budget. This is your tax money. Here's what you need to know.

Key Question: Has the city provided a full, line-item breakdown accessible to regular citizens? Or is the budget buried in bureaucratic language designed to discourage scrutiny?

What We Know So Far

The city has scheduled a public hearing to discuss the proposed budget for fiscal year 2026/2027. Public hearings are a legal requirement, but the question is always: how much genuine transparency accompanies them?

Here are the questions every citizen should be asking:

  1. Where are the line-item details? A budget summary is not accountability. Citizens deserve to see exactly where every dollar goes.
  2. How does spending compare to prior years? Year-over-year comparisons reveal trends that one-time snapshots hide.
  3. What new positions or contracts are proposed? New spending often hides in personnel costs and service contracts that escape casual review.
  4. Are there fund transfers between departments? Moving money between funds can obscure where it ultimately ends up.
  5. What's the reserve fund status? Healthy reserves mean fiscal responsibility. Depleted reserves mean trouble ahead.

What Citizens Should Demand

Before any budget is approved, the public deserves:

  • A complete, searchable budget document available online
  • Year-over-year comparison charts for every department
  • Plain-language summaries that don't require an accounting degree
  • Adequate time between posting and the hearing for genuine public review
  • A mechanism for citizens to submit written questions before the hearing

Stay Engaged

We will continue to monitor the budget process and report our findings. If you have information about city spending, contracts, or financial decisions that warrant scrutiny, contact us at tips@exposemiamiok.com.

"A budget is a moral document, not just a financial one. It tells you what a government truly values, regardless of what it claims to value."

The public hearing is your opportunity to ask questions. Show up. Speak up. Your tax dollars are on the table.
