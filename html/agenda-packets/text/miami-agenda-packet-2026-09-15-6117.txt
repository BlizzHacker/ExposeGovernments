A. Update and Discussion on the Grace and Peace Ministry Shelter and Shelter Funding
Rick Aldridge provided the Council with an update on the Grace and Peace Ministry shelter. The shelter has been
open for nine (9) months and has served two hundred (200) people. Eighty-five percent (85%) of those served
have obtained jobs and housing. There are currently thirty (30) people including two (2) families. Starting
September 14th, the shelter will be shut down for two (2) weeks for cleaning and maintenance. They have an
agreement with Grand Lake Mental Health, and they serve approximately three thousand two hundred (3,200)
meals a month plus food baskets. Aldridge requested the Council to consider adding one dollar ($1.00) to each
utility bill to assist the shelter with their funding needs. They are searching for a company that can assist with IQ
testing.

AGENDA TITLE:          Discussion and Possible Action on the Updated Customer Service & Utility Billing Policy;
                       Authorize the City Manager to Sign


PRESENTER:             Matt Mosley

BACKGROUND:
The Customer Service & Utility Billing Policy was last updated in 2015. Since that time, staff has identified several
areas where updates and additional clarification are needed to better reflect current practices and provide
clearer guidance for both customers and Utility Billing staff. The proposed revisions are intended to modernize the
policy, clarify procedures and responsibilities, and promote greater consistency in the administration of utility
accounts and billing services. This document has been reviewed by the City's legal team.

Some of the changes being presented are as follows:
      Inclement Weather clarification
      Notification of Deceased Customer
      Late Payment Penalty clarification
      Dishonored Payment Fee and Collections clarification
      Inaccessible Meters
      Debt Payment Plan
      New Infrastructure
      Water and Sewer Adjustment for Leak

STAFFS RECOMMENDATION:
REVIEW AND APPROVE THE UPDATED CUSTOMER SERVICE & UTILITY BILLING POLICY

APPLICATION FOR SERVICE
     o Adds documentation requirements to show legal right of occupancy, such as a lease, deed, or other
         reasonable documentation.
     o Add specific documentation requirements for business and nonprofits.
     o Adds a formal process for transferring account ownership and removing individuals from an account.
              o Clarifies how the Security Deposit is handled when ownership is transferred.
SECURITY DEPOSITS
     o Updates the section to state that the security deposit requirements are governed by Ordinance
         Chapter 24.
     o Expands the listed alternatives to a standard cash deposit.
     o Adds the current ordinance-based deposit requirements for customers who are not in Good Standing.
     o Clarifies that a security deposit may be refunded or credited after 12 months in Good Standing or
         when service is discontinued, less any amounts still owed.
     o Clarifies Good Standing status.
LANDLORD AND MULTIPLE PROPERTY OWNER (MPO) ACCOUNTS
*THIS IS A NEW SECTION*
         a. Setting Accounts:
              If a Customer owns more than one (1) property they can apply for Residential Multiple-Property
              Owner (MPO) classification. This will allow the Customer to set a secure PIN number and remove
              or add properties to the contract and reinstate properties into their name at any time the PIN is
              provided.

     b. Auto Reinstate:
        A Landlord or MPO may fill out an agreement for Automatic Reinstate, which will allow the
        Landlord’s properties to automatically reinstate (set) utilities in their name upon disconnection of
        their tenants. All applicable fees and minimum monthly usage charges will apply.

CONNECT/DISCONNECT SERVICE AND TRANSFER OF SERVICE
   o Consolidates several sections from current policy
   o Clarifies that service will be connected after the application is approved and the required Security

   o Establishes clearer same-day processing timelines
   o Adds documentation and recordkeeping requirements for applications and transfers, such as current

   o Creates a formal transfer process
   o Allows service to remain active at both the old and new locations for up to two weeks during a transfer
INCLEMENT WEATHER – REQUEST FOR DISCONNECT BY RENTERS/ACCOUNTHOLDERS
*THIS IS A NEW SECTION*
When inclement weather is projected to be sustained continuously for a 24-hour period (below 32 degrees
Fahrenheit (32°F) and a “Winter Weather Watch/Warning” for the immediate area has been issued by the
National Weather Service, and a renter/Accountholder requests to Disconnect their utilities the following will
occur:
       a. Customer Service Office will conduct their normal process by verifying the Accountholder
       b. Customer Service Office will request the property and/or Property Manager’s phone number(s)
            from the renter
       c. Customer Service Office will attempt to contact all numbers to inquire if they would like to keep the
            utilities on and set an account in their name
       d. If the Landlord is reached, a recording of the phone conversation will be saved
       e. The CSR will document all phone calls attempted or completed on both the occupant side and
            address side of the account
       f. A reasonable amount of time will be given for the paperwork and any Security Deposit required to
            be submitted
       g. Metering will record the utility readings for the Accountholder with the Customer Service Office
       h. The utilities will NOT be turned off until the temperature reaches above 32 degrees Fahrenheit at
            9:00AM or until the warning/watch is cancelled with the only exception being if the owner/property
            manager wants it turned off.
DENIAL OF SERVICE TO A CUSTOMER
   o Adds an express opportunity for a customer with an unpaid balance to enter into a Debt Payment Plan
       (DPP).
   o Clarifies that service may be denied when the landlord has an outstanding bill at the same property for
       which a new applicant is requesting service.
   o Updates requirements to be in accordance with Ordinance Chapter 24 regarding Security Deposits
       when moving to a new location with a delinquent balance.
NOTIFICATION OF DECEASED CUSTOMER
    o Adds a process allowing a surviving spouse who was not listed on the account to assume the account
        without an additional Security Deposit when the account is in Good Standing.
    o Changes the responsibility period for those remaining lawful occupants from beginning at the date of
        death to beginning with the last payment in full on the account.
STATEMENT FOR SERVICE RENDERED
   o Clarifies that a customer’s first or final bill may cover less than a full 30-day billing period.
   o Adds a requirement that when a significant meter-reading discrepancy is identified, a second reread
       will be completed before billing to verify the reading and reduce the chance of issuing a large incorrect
       charge or credit.
ESTIMATED BILLS
   o Updates terminology.
   o Establishes the formula for estimated bills: most recently billed month’s consumption (i.e. January
       2021) plus the month’s consumption prior to that (i.e. December 2020) plus last year’s consumption of
       the recently billed month (i.e. January 2020), and then divides that total consumption by three (3).
       (i.e. January 2021 + December 2020 + January 2020 ÷ 3 = Estimated Bill)
AVERAGE MONTHLY PLAN
    o Clarifies how the AMP is calculated when an account has less than 12 months of billing history.

    o   Adds a specific restriction that an account with an active Debt Payment Plan (DPP) cannot also be
        placed on AMP until the DPP has been paid off.
   o Adds that a customer leaving AMP may be eligible for an extended DPP to pay any remaining AMP
        balance.
   o Updates terminology.
PAYMENT OPTIONS
   o Adds current electronic payment options.
   o Adds formal utility assistance pledge process and establishes what information must be included in a
       pledge letter.
LATE PAYMENT PENALTY
   o Updates terminology.
   o Expands language to make it clear that the penalty applies not only to water, sewer, and electric
        charges, but also to solid waste and other services billed by MSUA.
DISHONORED PAYMENT FEE AND COLLECTION
   o Expands the former returned-check process to cover all dishonored payments.
   o Generally allows one week for payment by cash or money order before service is interrupted.
   o Adds a two-week arrangement when a resource provider pledges to cover the dishonored amount and
       fee.
   o Adds specific procedures for situations such as dishonored reconnect payments.
   o Adds a path for customers to return to normal payment methods after being on a Cash Only status.
   o Adds an exception clarifying that if an ACH payment is replaced before the ACH is actually returned,
       the customer will not be subject to the dishonored-payment process.
INTERRUPTION OF SERVICE FOR FAILURE TO PAY (SONP)
   o Adds a more detailed customer-notification process.
   o Formalizes the review of the interruption queue and identifies situations in which an account may be
       removed, including balances under $20, active assistance pledges, pending leak adjustments, etc.
   o Adds courtesy-notification process for business accounts.
   o Clarifies that partial payments in the field are not accepted.
   o Clarifies final-billing and restoration requirements.
   o Documents the current $55 interruption and $55 restore Dispatch Fees.
INTERRUPT FOR NON-PAY (SONP) DURING EXTREME WEATHER AND GOVERNMENT SHUTDOWN
   o Replaces the older weather-warning language with specific National Weather Service-based
       thresholds.
   o Clarifies that when weather conditions prevent interruption, the process is postponed until the next
       business day when conditions allow it to proceed.
   o Adds a specific provision stating that a government shutdown does not suspend interruption of service
       or penalty procedures.
OPPORTUNITY TO BE HEARD FOR A DEBT PAYMENT PLAN (DPP)
   o Establishes that multiple Debt Payment Plans (DPP) cannot run at the same time.
   o Clarifies that customer on the Average Monthly Plan (AMP) cannot simultaneously enter into a DPP.
   o Establishes standard plan requirements.
   o Adds a standardized repayment schedule.
NOTICE OF INACCESSIBLE METER AND INSPECTION OF MSUA PROPERTY
   o Expands the former provision into a more structured three-step process.
   o Replaces older general 60-day inaccessible-meter language with a clearer month-by-month escalation
       process.
CUSTOMER RESPONSIBILITY FOR METER (WATER AND ELECTRIC)
   o Updates terminology.
   o Adds clearer internal procedure that when a customer reports a meter issue, the Metering
        Department is sent to investigate.
ARREAR ACCOUNTS AND REFUND ON ACCOUNTS
   o Changes reporting schedule from monthly to biannual, with reports in July and December.
WAIVERS
*THIS IS A NEW SECTION*
       a. Any Customer may request a waiver for fees associated with their account.

       c. The City/Trust Manager or their designee has the final say in the approval or denial therein.
       d. All approved waivers will be added to the packet for the next regular session Miami Special Utility
            Authority meeting.
REMOVAL OR REMOVAL AND REPLACEMENT OF ELECTRIC SERVICE LINE
    o Clarifies that the request must be made through the Customer Service Office at least 24 hours in
       advance.
    o Adds specific situations in which the electric service line removal fee will not be charged.
NEW INFRASTRUCTURE BUILDS
*THIS IS A NEW SECTION*
All new infrastructure shall require a Contribution in Aid of Construction (CIAC) prior to installation unless
waived by the City/Trust Manager. If service is ever terminated, the infrastructure will remain the property of
MSUA. The CIAC may be made in the following ways:
       a. One-time Payment
            Made to MSUA prior to the installation of any new infrastructure that must be installed by MSUA.
            This payment is non-refundable.

       b. Customer Installed
          If new infrastructure is to be added by a Customer, that Customer will be required to install the
          infrastructure and be held liable to maintain it until the following requirements are met:
              i. Fully completed.
              ii. Passes inspection.
              iii. Written acceptance by MSUA.
                             Once MSUA accepts the new infrastructure, MSUA will own, maintain, and
                               repair that infrastructure.

PRIVATE AREA LIGHTING PROCEDURE
*THIS IS A NEW SECTION*
Customers may request to disconnect, install, or reconnect an area light
       a. Disconnect
              i. While setting new service, the Accountholder may select to disconnect an already existing
                   area light at the service address. The CSR will remove the area light from the account
                   while setting up the new utility service.
              ii. Accountholders who have had an area light installed may choose to disconnect the area
                   light from their account after the required term length as established by Ordinance Chapter
                   24.
                             Area lights that are disconnected prematurely, in relation to the original
                               agreement term length, will require all unpaid charges be calculated and
                               applied to the next utility billing Statement.

       b. Install and Reconnect
              i. Accountholders may request an area light to be installed or reconnected at their service
                   address.
              ii. An Area Lighting Service Agreement must be completed and signed, and any charges
                   established by Ordinance Chapter 24 must be paid prior to installation or reconnection.
                        Area Lighting Service Agreements signed for a light set on an existing utility pole
                           will require a term length of one (1) year.
                        Area Lighting Service Agreements signed for a light set on a newly installed utility
                           pole will require a term length of three (3) years.

                                                                                                                  45
                           Term fulfillment of the Area Lighting Service Agreement will result in the transition
                            of a month-to-month agreement until a request for disconnection of the area light is
                            made, at which time, a new Area Lighting Service Agreement must be established
                            before the area light can be reconnected.
                iii. A work order will be generated by the Customer Service Office and issued for completion
                     by the electric service crew as requested on the agreement.
                iv. Monthly billing will commence upon completion of the work order and will continue until a
                     disconnect request is made by the Accountholder.
                                 A Landlord may request to install or reconnect an area light on a rental
                                     property they own and have the monthly charge added to the utility service
                                     Bill for the service address at which they reside. If the Landlord does not
                                     reside within the City of Miami servicing area, this would not be applicable.
WATER METER TAP
    o Clarifies that the water tap fee is based on the size of the meter requested and is charged in
       accordance with Ordinance Chapter 24.
    o Adds a defined internal work-order process.
SEASONAL METER
*THIS IS A NEW SECTION*
Any Customer requesting a seasonal meter will need a permit from Code Compliance and will be charged a
seasonal meter fee based on the size of the meter being requested per Ordinance Chapter 24. A work order
containing the location, name, meter size, cost, and date of service requested, will be filled out by a CSR and
placed in the utility department’s mailbox as well as an email sent to the utility department’s water crew.
       a. A seasonal meter will only be authorized for use in situations where the water passing through the
            meter will not be introduced into the sewer system.
       b. The Miami Special Utility Authority reserves the right to refuse installation of a seasonal meter at
            any time.
HYDRANT METER
*THIS IS A NEW SECTION*
       a. Any Customer requesting a hydrant flow meter will be required to pay a deposit for that meter per
            Ordinance Chapter 24. They will be required to fill out a business application with the approximate
            location of the hydrant being utilized.
       b. The Customer Service Office will create an address for that hydrant (if not already in the database)
            and the deposit will be applied to the account.
       c. Once the hydrant meter has been returned and inspected, the deposit will be returned to the
            Customer. Any hydrant meter repairs required due to damage will be deducted from the deposit
            based on the cost of the repair.
WATER AND SEWER CHARGE ADJUSTMENTS FOR LEAK
    o Allows a 100% adjustment of the sewer charge once per calendar year per location when the leak
       required a permit and inspection and the billed water usage is above the customer’s normal usage.
    o Establishes how normal usage is determined.
    o Adds specific eligibility requirements
            o Customer must have suffered a water Leak on their premise.
            o Toilets, faucets, water tanks, and swimming pools do not qualify for adjustment.
            o The leak must be repaired and the adjustment requested within three months.
            o Receipts or proof of repair must be provided.

INTERRUPT FOR NON-PAY (SONP) DURING
EXTREME WEATHER AND GOVERNMENT
SHUTDOWN
     a. Summer Months
        At 8:30AM the day of interruption for non-
        pay, if the National Weather Service
        (NWS) issues a local forecast for the
        Miami area predicting the heat index will
        be one hundred degrees Fahrenheit
        (100°F) or higher on the day of
        disconnection, or if the actual heat index is
        one hundred and one degrees Fahrenheit
        (101°F) or higher, the utility shall suspend
        its disconnection of service activity until
        the next business day when weather
        conditions permit the process to proceed.

       b. Winter Months
          At 8:30AM the day of interruption for non-
          pay, if the NWS issues a local forecast for
          the Miami area predicting the temperature
          will drop below thirty-two degrees
          Fahrenheit (32°F) for any time period
          during the following twenty-four (24) hours,
          the utility shall suspend its disconnection
          of service activity until the next business
          day when weather conditions permit the
          process to proceed.

       c. Government Shutdown
          During government shutdown, interruption
          of service and penalty procedures will
          remain in place.

DEFINITIONS
Accountholder – The person or persons legally responsible for payment of a utility account (see also
Customer).
Automatic Reinstate – The restoration of utility service without the requirement of a new application,
additional Security Deposit, or manual processing, when a Customer satisfies all conditions for reconnection,
including payment of all past due amounts and applicable fees.
Average Monthly Plan (AMP) – The optional billing program available upon Customer request that calculates
a Customer’s monthly Bill based on the average of the previous twelve (12) months of billing. The average is
recalculated on a rolling basis to reflect the most recent usage. To offset fluctuations between actual monthly
charges and the averaged Bill amount, a reserve balance is maintained on the account. This reserve may
increase or decrease as necessary to maintain a consistent monthly payment. Upon termination of the AMP or
closure of the account, the reserve balance shall be reconciled, either being billed to the Customer or credited
to the account.
Bill – The monthly statement of account, payable as rendered on a regularly designated date to each Customer
(can also be referred to as invoice, statement, and utility bill).
Billing Period – The billing period may be referred to as “month” or “billing month”. The intent is to represent a
period of approximately thirty (30) days between regular meter readings irrespective of calendar months. The
first or final Bill may be for services spanning less than thirty (30) days.
Contribution in Aid to Construction – One-time payment or property donation made to a utility company by a
Customer or developer to fund new infrastructure or upgrade services.
City Attorney – The licensed attorney appointed or retained by the City Council to serve as chief legal
counsel, responsible for providing legal advice and representation to the City/MSUA in matters pertaining to
municipal operations and enforcement of ordinances.
City/MSUA Cost – The amount the City and/or MSUA will bill out, which will include materials, labor, and
equipment costs plus any administrative costs as established by Ordinance Chapter 24.
City/Trust Manager – The person responsible for the administration of the City of Miami, which includes the
MSUA.
Code – The City-approved electrical and plumbing code.
Connected Load – The combined nominal rated capacity of all the connected electrical motors, appliances,
devices, and other current consuming equipment installed on the Customer’s premises, which may be
operated or supplied with energy at the will of the Customer.
Critical Care List – Allows for a Customer who is on life sustaining equipment to be a top priority for reinstating
power during a power outage. This also gives the Customer an extra 30 days to make payment prior to being
shut off for non-payment.
Customer – An individual, firm, or corporation receiving a service and/or commodity and/or making a payment
to the City of Miami or MSUA for other services or fees (i.e. court fines, building permit fees, animal control
fees, etc.) [see also Accountholder].
Customer Service Office – The office tasked with creating utility Bills and processing citizens payments.
Customer Service Representative (CSR) – The individual tasked with processing citizens payments and
answering questions related to utility Bills or City/MSUA functions.
Customer Service Manager – The individual tasked with supervising the operations of the Customer Service
Office, overseeing the Customer Service Representatives and Utility Billing Clerk, ensuring compliance with
applicable ordinances and policies, and administering billing, collection, and Customer account procedures.

Debt Payment Plan (DPP) – A contract between a Customer and MSUA that allows a Customer to move a
balance from their account to a contract. The contract is paid off over a period of time based on the amount of
the contract.
Demand (kW) – Whenever maximum demand is mentioned in any schedule or is taken in the applications of
any schedule, it is the Customer’s highest fifteen (15) minute demand (kW) for power delivered by the MSUA to
each point of service of the Customer measured during the monthly Billing Period.
Disconnect – Permanent stoppage of all utilities.
Dishonored Payment – When a negotiable instrument (check, money order, account draft, any electronic
transfer, debit/credit card, etc.) given in payment is dishonored as established by Ordinance Chapter 27.
Dispatch Fees – There are six (6) types of Dispatch Fees: connect, interrupt, restore, connect after business
hours, restore after business hours, and re-read, as established by Ordinance Chapter 24.
Good Standing – A Customer who has had twelve (12) consecutive monthly payments on their account which
could span over one (1) or more account(s), within the last year, on which there have been no Dishonored
Payments, no collection efforts initiated, and no interruption of services to their account(s).
Inspector – The City electrical and plumbing inspector or a duly authorized representative of the utility
department.
Interrupt for Non-Pay (SONP) – A temporary interruption of electric service until the past due Bill and all fees
are paid in full.
Kilowatt Hour or kWh – A measurement of electrical energy used or consumed (1000 watts used for one hour,
or equivalent) as registered on standard meters.
Landlord – The owner of any one of the following: residence, lessor or sublessor of any dwelling unit or the
building of which it is a part, manufactured or mobile home sites, spaces or lots.
Leak – Water consumption caused by broken, damaged, or faulty plumbing.
Material Cost – The amount the City and/or MSUA will bill out, which will include material costs plus any
administrative costs as established by Ordinance Chapter 24.
Metering Department – The department of the MSUA responsible for the installation, maintenance, testing,
calibration, reading, and replacement of water and electric meters, and for ensuring the accuracy and integrity
of metered consumption data.
Metering Serviceman – An employee or authorized representative of the MSUA assigned to install, remove,
test, repair, read, and maintain utility meters and related equipment.
Miami Special Utility Authority (MSUA) – The Trust which oversees all utility-related operations.
Residential Multiple-Property Owner (MPO) – Landlord of multiple residential properties being provided
residential service.
Phase – The electrical definition, used herein to designate type of service available, such as single-phase or
three-phase.
Property Manager – A person authorized to conduct business on behalf of the property owner by legible
instrument in which the owner’s signature is verified by identification or notary.
Rate/Rate Schedule – The rates published by Ordinance Chapter 24, which applies to the commodity being
considered.
Right of Entry – The MSUA reserves the right to enter upon the grounds of any property for the
purpose of installing, maintaining, or reading water and electrical meters, and it shall be unlawful for any owner,
occupant or lessee of any premises to prevent or to refuse to permit such entry. (Ordinance Chapter 24-3)

CUSTOMER SERVICE POLICIES
       1. Customer Interactions
             a. Individuals Requesting Account Information
                To request information about an account, a Customer must provide at least one of the
                following:
                    i.  The last four (4) digits of the social security number on the account
                   ii.  The account number
                  iii.  Power of Attorney over the account holder
                  iv.   Court documents naming the individual as the Personal Representative of the estate
                        along with the Letters of Testamentary or Letters of Administration

                b. Customer Complaints
                   If a Customer has a complaint and would like to escalate their complaint to the next level,
                   we will follow the chain of command to satisfy or remedy the complaint.

                c. Critical Care
                   Customers with a life-threatening condition that are on life-sustaining equipment which
                   requires continuous electrical supply may be eligible to be placed on the Critical Care List.
                   There will be two main factors considered when seeking eligibility:
                      i. Equipment must be prescribed by an Advanced Practice Provider (APP).
                     ii. Note from APP stating that the Customers’ life is dependent on the equipment and that
                         without power the equipment will not operate.

       2. Applications for Service
             a. Starting New Account:
                 Before receiving service, every Customer must complete and sign a written application or
                 contract using the standard MSUA form. This agreement will outline the applicable service
                 Rates and will be governed by MSUA’s current policies and procedures. The application
                 requires the Customer to provide a current lease, deed, or other reasonable documentation
                 to show legal right of occupancy. Documentation must be unaltered unless approved by
                 Customer Service Manager. Business application requirements must include a copy of the
                 LLC Articles of Organization. If a business is Not for Profit, then the Articles of Incorporation
                 will be required.

                    An application and contract for services with The City of Miami/MSUA shall be signed by the
                    Customer.

       7. Inclement Weather – Request for Disconnect by Renters/Accountholders
          When inclement weather is projected to be sustained continuously for a 24-hour period (below 32
          degrees Fahrenheit (32°F) and a “Winter Weather Watch/Warning” for the immediate area has been
          issued by the National Weather Service, and a renter/Accountholder requests to Disconnect their
          utilities the following will occur:
                a. Customer Service Office will conduct their normal process by verifying the Accountholder.
                b. Customer Service Office will request the property and/or Property Manager’s phone
                    number(s) from the renter.
                c. Customer Service Office will attempt to contact all numbers to inquire if they would like to
                    keep the utilities on and set an account in their name.
                d. If the Landlord is reached, a recording of the phone conversation will be saved.
                e. The CSR will document all phone calls attempted or completed on both the occupant side
                    and address side of the account.
                f. A reasonable amount of time will be given for the paperwork and any Security Deposit
                    required to be submitted.
                g. Metering will record the utility readings for the Accountholder with the Customer Service
                    Office.
                h. The utilities will NOT be turned off until the temperature reaches above 32 degrees
                    Fahrenheit at 9:00AM or until the warning/watch is cancelled, with the only exception being
                    if the owner/property manager wants it turned off.

       8. Denial of Service to a Customer
          A Customer may be refused service when there is an unpaid account for services previously
          provided. However, there is an opportunity to execute a debt payment plan agreement (see
          “Opportunity to be Heard for a Debt Payment Plan”).

          The Customer Service Office shall not be required to provide service to an applicant who uses an
          alias, trade name, business name, or the name of a relative or other person as a device to escape
          payment of an unpaid obligation. The Customer Service Office also is not required to provide
          service when there is a reasonable suspicion that the applicant is changing utility service from one
          occupant to another occupant of the same residence to avoid payment of an unpaid obligation; in
          such case, the matter shall be reviewed and resolved by the City/Trust Manager in consultation with
          the City Attorney.

          Service will be denied if the Landlord of the property has an outstanding Bill at the property that the
          new applicant is applying for service.

          No person, persons, groups, or businesses can move from one location, with an unpaid Bill, and
          have service connected in a new location. If any person has a delinquent account, they shall be
          required to pay the entire Bill in full, plus establish a Security Deposit per Ordinance Chapter 24.

       9. Notification of Deceased Customer
             a. If the deceased person was the only one living on the premises:
                  Contracts terminate upon the date of death of the deceased applicant, although the debt for
                  services rendered remains enforceable against the decedent’s estate. Further, after the

       10. Statement for Service Rendered
           A Statement shall be mailed to each Customer, or to such person as they shall direct, once a month
           for service rendered the preceding month. The Statement shall show date of reading, meter
           readings, Rate classification, consumption, and net charges. The first and final billing Statements
           may be for services spanning less than thirty (30) days.

          In rare instances when a discrepancy is identified during the initial meter reading, a second reread
          shall be conducted prior to billing to ensure accuracy. This additional verification step is
          implemented to prevent the issuance of significant billing adjustments – either large credits or
          charges – without confirmation from a third-party.

          The failure of any Customer to receive a Statement for any utility charges shall not excuse the
          Customer from their obligation to pay such charges within the time specified by City Ordinance.

       11. Estimated Bills
           If any meter shall stop, or for any reason fails to register properly, or upon failure to read the meter,
           the Utility Billing Clerk shall estimate the monthly Bill. Whenever possible, estimated Bills shall be
           based on the average consumption as calculated by the billing software as follows: to estimate
           consumption, our software system uses the most recently billed month’s consumption (i.e. January
           2021) plus the month’s consumption prior to that (i.e. December 2020) plus last year’s consumption
           of the recently billed month (i.e. January 2020), and then divides that total consumption by three (3).

          (i.e. January 2021 + December 2020 + January 2020 ÷ 3 = Estimated Bill)

                   business day.

                          be interrupted.

       18. Interrupt for Non-Pay (SONP) During Extreme Weather and Government Shutdown
               a. Summer Months
                   At 8:30AM the day of interruption for non-pay, if the National Weather Service (NWS) issues
                   a local forecast for the Miami area predicting the heat index will be one hundred degrees
                   Fahrenheit (100°F) or higher on the day of disconnection, or if the actual heat index is one
                   hundred and one degrees Fahrenheit (101°F) or higher, the utility shall suspend its
                   disconnection of service activity until the next business day when weather conditions permit
                   the process to proceed.

               b. Winter Months
                  At 8:30AM the day of interruption for non-pay, if the NWS issues a local forecast for the
                  Miami area predicting the temperature will drop below thirty-two degrees Fahrenheit (32°F)
                  for any time period during the following twenty-four (24) hours, the utility shall suspend its
                  disconnection of service activity until the next business day when weather conditions permit
                  the process to proceed.

               c. Government Shutdown
                  During government shutdown, interruption of service and penalty procedures will remain in
                  place.

WATER POLICIES

  1. Water Meter Tap
     Any Customer requesting a water tap will be charged a water tap fee based on the size of the meter
     being requested per Ordinance Chapter 24. A work order containing the location, name, meter size,
     cost, and date of service requested, will be filled out by a CSR and placed in the utility department’s
     mailbox as well as an email sent to the utility department’s water crew.

  2. Seasonal Meter
     Any Customer requesting a seasonal meter will need a permit from Code Compliance and will be
     charged a seasonal meter fee based on the size of the meter being requested per Ordinance Chapter
     24. A work order containing the location, name, meter size, cost, and date of service requested, will be
     filled out by a CSR and placed in the utility department’s mailbox as well as an email sent to the utility
     department’s water crew.
          a. A seasonal meter will only be authorized for use in situations where the water passing through
              the meter will not be introduced into the sewer system.
          b. The MSUA reserves the right to refuse installation of a seasonal meter at any time.

  3. Hydrant Meter
        a. Any Customer requesting a hydrant flow meter will be required to pay a deposit for that meter
           per Ordinance Chapter 24. They will be required to fill out a business application with the
           approximate location of the hydrant being utilized.
        b. The Customer Service Office will create an address for that hydrant (if not already in the
           database) and the deposit will be applied to the account.
        c. Once the hydrant meter has been returned and inspected, the deposit will be returned to the
           Customer. Any hydrant meter repairs required due to damage will be deducted from the deposit
           based on the cost of the repair.

  4. Water and Sewer Charge Adjustments for Leak
     As a convenience only, for our Customers, a door hanger, postcard, or letter will be issued if a Leak is
     detected.

       Adjustments for Leaks will be as follows:
             a. A Customer experiencing a water Leak that requires a permit and inspection may be granted
                a 100% adjustment of the sewer charge once per calendar year per location, provided the
                water consumption for the Billing Period is higher than normal when compared to the
                Customer’s average usage over the previous three (3) months.

                 If a Customer has not occupied the location for a minimum of three (3) months, the Customer
                 Service Manager shall determine normal usage by calculating the average consumption of
                 the most recent five (5) accounts at that location.
              b. To apply for an adjustment, a Customer must meet the following criteria:
                    i. Customer must have suffered a water Leak on their premise. Toilets, faucets, water
                       tanks, and swimming pools do not qualify for adjustment.
                   ii. Customer must have had the Leak repaired within three (3) months of
                       discovery/notification and request the adjustment within the same time.
