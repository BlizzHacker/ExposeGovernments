Open Records · Austin, TX

File a request the city has to answer

The Texas Public Information Act (Tex. Gov't Code ch. 552) gives you a right to the City of Austin's records. You do not have to say why you want them, and in Texas you do not have to be a resident. Build the letter below, send it, and start the clock.

⏰

The deadline

§ 552.301 — the body has ten (10) business days. Write the date you sent it on the letter. A missed deadline is only useful if you can prove when the clock started.

💵

The fees

§ 552.2615. Ask for an estimate above $40.00 before anything is copied, and ask for electronic delivery — a PDF has no per-page charge to argue about.

⚖

If they say no

Texas has no free citizen appeal board. If the City wants to withhold, it must ask the Attorney General for a ruling within ten business days (§ 552.301) and send you a copy of what it asked — that request and the ruling are both public, and they are the best record of what a city tried to hide. If the City simply never answers, a complaint goes to the county or district attorney under § 552.3215, or you can sue for mandamus under § 552.321.

Build your letter

This runs entirely in your browser. Nothing you type here is sent anywhere, stored, or seen by us — copy the result and send it yourself from your own email. 


      

    


    

      Write the request narrow, then widen it
      

        “All emails about the project” gets refused as overbroad and burns your
        deadline. “Emails between the City Manager and [named party] between 1 March
        and 30 April” gets answered. Once you see what came back, ask again with what
        you learned. Two narrow requests beat one that never gets filled.
      

    

  






  

    
Where to send it

    

      Austin's own records channel is listed on the
      City Desk, with the exact URL the City
      publishes. Use it — a request filed through the body's own portal is the hardest one
      for it to say it never received.
    

    

      Send a copy to foia@moveweight.com at the same time. We log the send
      date, watch the deadline, and if it lapses we publish that fact. A request nobody
      knows about is a request the city can ignore quietly.
    

  






  

    
Track the clock

    

      § 552.301 gives the City ten (10) business days. Put the send
      date in your calendar with that many days added, and put a second reminder a week
      later. Most requests are not denied — they are simply never answered, and the only
      thing that changes that is somebody noticing on the exact day.
    

  






  

    
If you are refused

    
Texas has no free citizen appeal board. If the City wants to withhold, it must ask the Attorney General for a ruling within ten business days (§ 552.301) and send you a copy of what it asked — that request and the ruling are both public, and they are the best record of what a city tried to hide. If the City simply never answers, a complaint goes to the county or district attorney under § 552.3215, or you can sue for mandamus under § 552.321.

    

      Keep the refusal
      

        Whatever the outcome, keep the exact wording of the denial and the date. A refusal
        that cites no statute, or cites one that does not apply, is itself the story — and
        it is the document an appeal turns on.
