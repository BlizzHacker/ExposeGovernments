Six findings, with the working shown

Ordered by how checkable they are. The first three are the City's own words or votes. The last three are arithmetic on published figures — our contribution, and we show it so you can verify rather than trust us.

1. The City states no independent impact study was ever conducted

“No independent impact study has been conducted.” City of San Angelo, official Data Center Information page

This is the City answering its own FAQ about a 1.2‑gigawatt facility on land it sold. It is not a characterisation by us or by opponents.

3. No executed Chapter 380 agreement has ever been published

The City says “to date, there have been no incentives or subsidies offered,” while confirming Chapter 380 terms remain to be negotiated. When draft documents naming Emergent circulated publicly in June 2026, the City's position was that they were examples and that no terms had been decided. Separately, reporting in April 2026 concluded the project will qualify for a large state incentive regardless of local action.

The checkable claim

As of 25 July 2026 there is no publicly available executed 380 agreement for this project. Every economic figure in circulation is therefore a projection, not a term sheet. We have a records request drafted for the negotiation file.

5. Our arithmetic The recall threshold for District 3 is 47 signatures — because 155 people voted

San Angelo sets a recall petition threshold at 30% of votes cast in the last election for that seat. That produces thresholds two orders of magnitude apart on the same council:

47 signatures can force a recall election in District 3. That is a story about municipal turnout as much as about the data center, and the cost of the election falls on all taxpayers either way. City Clerk Heather Stastny certifies the petitions.

What we are and are not claiming

We are not claiming corruption. Nothing on this site alleges a crime by any official. Where we describe an allegation we attribute it to whoever made it.

We are not anti-data-center. The City adopted mandatory closed-loop cooling and banned evaporative cooling, 7–0. That is the strongest water protection available to a city in this position and we say so on our own water page.

Our objection is to sequence and disclosure — a binding vote 126 days before the rules, and an unpublished 380 agreement underneath every economic projection.

We correct errors on the page, not quietly. Any official named on this site may send a response of reasonable length and we will publish it in full, unedited.
