Data Center Watch

In roughly seven months, San Angelo rezoned 343–374 acres of city-owned land, authorized its sale to a Dallas developer, and wrote three brand-new ordinances to govern a 1.2‑gigawatt data center campus — without commissioning an independent study of what it would do to the city's water or power. This is the record, in order, with every source.

The project in brief

Figures as represented by the City of San Angelo and the developers. Where the City's numbers and reported numbers differ, both are shown.
Developers Skybox Datacenters (Dallas) and Emergent Data Centers
Location Northeast San Angelo, U.S. Highway 67 N at City Farm Road — the former city farm
Land City-owned, 343–374 acres
Buildings Up to six, phased 18–24 months each
Power Reported at up to 1.2 GW for the full complex; earlier reporting cited 600 MW. AEP Texas is the provider; a developer-funded dedicated substation is planned. Final load is set by ERCOT studies, not by the City.
Claimed valuation $21–22 billion, against a total city property tax base of $7.9 billion
Jobs claimed 30–60 permanent in year one; 120–200 at full build-out over ten years; wages stated as $75,000–$110,000
Independent impact study None. “No independent impact study has been conducted.” — City of San Angelo
Sources: City of San Angelo — Data Center Information · SanAngeloLive — The Skybox Datacenter Controversy · DataCenterDynamics · SanAngeloLive — Recall of San Angelo's Mayor Fails
Discrepancy we are chasing

Reported capacity for this campus ranges from 600 MW (December 2025) to 1.2 GW (June 2026) — a doubling with no public council action recorded in between explaining it. For scale: the AEP Red Creek substation serving the area is rated around 250 MW, and the entire city of San Angelo uses roughly 10% of it. We have an open records request drafted on the ERCOT interconnection correspondence. Help fund it →

Timeline

Every dated action we can document. Vote tallies are shown where recorded.

  1. May 2025
    Council authorizes the city manager to negotiate the land sale Unanimous

    City Council votes unanimously to authorize the city manager to negotiate a purchase and sale agreement with Skybox Datacenters for the city-owned tract northeast of town. This is the decision that put 343–374 acres of public land into play, and it happened months before most residents had heard the project named.

  2. December 2, 2025
    Site rezoned: Ranch & Estate → Light Manufacturing

    First reading of the zone change carries. Rezoning from Ranch & Estate — the classification that had kept the old city farm rural — to Light Manufacturing is the single legal step that makes a hyperscale data center possible on this land.

  3. December 29, 2025
    Residents call their own meeting

    With no city-hosted public forum scheduled, residents organize an impromptu meeting at Stephens Central Library. This is the origin of the organized opposition that becomes the San Angelo Data Center Citizen Coalition.

  4. January 13, 2026
    Zone change approved on final reading 6–1

    Second reading passes 6–1. The land is now zoned for the project. Note what does not exist at this point: any adopted ordinance limiting the facility's water use, its wastewater discharge, or its noise. Those come later — after the zoning is already locked in.

  5. May 5, 2026
    First discussion of land use regulations

    Council takes up proposed data center land use regulations — nearly four months after approving the zoning.

  6. May 19, 2026
    Land use ordinance adopted 6–0

    Second reading of the land use regulations passes, plus first readings on water usage and wastewater discharge. Sets a 100 ft minimum front setback to residential zoning, a 75 ft maximum height, and a stated sound target of 55 dBA at residential property lines.

    Read the land use ordinance (PDF, City of San Angelo)

  7. May 29, 2026
    Recall affidavit filed against Councilmember Joe Self (District 2)

    The Data Center Citizen Coalition files a recall affidavit against District 2's Joe Self. The threshold for his district is 385 valid signatures.

  8. June 2, 2026
    Sewer / discharge ordinance adopted 5–1

    The wastewater discharge ordinance passes on a split vote — the closest of the three.

    Read the sewer/discharge ordinance (PDF, City of San Angelo)

  9. June 4, 2026
    The recalls' cost to taxpayers becomes a story

    Reporting notes that recall elections are paid for out of city funds — a cost residents bear regardless of which side of the data center they are on.

    SanAngeloLive — Recall Petitions Will Be Costly for San Angelo Taxpayers

  10. June 9, 2026
    City: circulating 380 agreement documents are “only examples”

    As draft Chapter 380 economic development agreement documents involving Emergent circulate online, the City issues a clarification: the documents are examples, no terms have been decided, and no agreement has been reached. The City's project page simultaneously states that “to date, there have been no incentives or subsidies offered,” while confirming 380 terms remain to be negotiated.

    SanAngeloLive — San Angelo Clarifies No Agreement Reached

  11. June 16, 2026
    Water use ordinance adopted 7–0

    Council unanimously adopts the water use regulations, then moves to begin negotiations with Skybox and Emergent over the campus itself. Requirements: closed-loop cooling only, no evaporative cooling, initial fill capped at 8.0 gallons per square foot of gross building area within 12 months of the certificate of occupancy, annual makeup capped at 3% of loop volume per building, drain-and-refill no more than every three years, and cooling water metered separately.

    Read the water usage ordinance (PDF, City of San Angelo) · SanAngeloLive · ConchoValleyHomepage

  12. June 22, 2026
    Mayoral recall fails on signature count

    The petition to recall Mayor Tom Thompson — filed over what organizers called insufficient transparency during negotiations to sell city land to Emergent/Skybox — falls short. City Clerk Heather Stastny reports fewer than 1,900 signatures submitted against a requirement of 2,971.

    SanAngeloLive — Recall of San Angelo's Mayor Fails

  13. July 15, 2026
    Recall petition filed against Councilmember Harry Thomas (District 3)

    The Coalition files with the City Clerk, saying it gathered substantially more than the 47 signatures required in District 3 — a threshold that low because only 155 votes were cast in the May 2023 District 3 election. Filed almost two weeks before the July 28 deadline. Thomas: “I have no plans to resign from my position.” Under city ordinance, if he does not resign within 15 days of formal notice, Council must order a recall election within 30–90 days. His term runs to May 2027.

    SanAngeloLive — Recall Petition Submitted · Thomas Says He Has No Plans to Resign

  14. July 24, 2026
    Texas Agriculture Commissioner urges a halt to data center growth

    The state Agriculture Commissioner publicly calls for slowing data center expansion in Texas — a state-level intervention landing while San Angelo is mid-negotiation.

    SanAngeloLive — Ag Commissioner Urges Halt to Data Center Growth

The water arithmetic

The City's position is that a closed-loop facility uses almost no water once running. Its own worked example: a 250,000 sq ft building takes roughly 2 million gallons one time to fill, then about 60,000 gallons a year in makeup water.

Earlier reporting put it at 1.5 million gallons per building — about 9 million gallons across six — plus roughly 15,000 gallons a day ongoing. San Angelo produces about 20 million gallons a day.

What the ordinance does and does not do

The cap is written per square foot of building, not as a hard ceiling on the campus. Six buildings at the 8.0 gal/sq ft limit scale with however large those buildings are permitted to be. The ordinance also permits a full drain-and-refill every three years, which resets the initial-fill volume each time.

What we have not seen — and what no ordinance requires — is a published projection of total campus water demand at full build-out, measured against the city's ~41 months of supply. See the full water picture →

Sources: City of San Angelo — Data Center Information · SanAngeloLive

The tax question

A $21–22 billion facility added to a $7.9 billion tax base would nearly triple the city's taxable value on paper. That is the entire economic case, and it is a real one.

The complication is what gets abated. The City says no incentives have been offered to date, while confirming that Chapter 380 terms remain to be negotiated. Separately, reporting in April 2026 concluded the project will qualify for a large state tax incentive regardless of what the City does — which raises the question of how much of that $21–22 billion actually reaches San Angelo's tax rolls, and in which years.

The City's own materials put the expected financial benefit in “year 5 or 6.”

Open question

No executed 380 agreement has been published. Until one is, the public cannot evaluate the deal's terms — only its projections. We are filing for the negotiation records. Fund that request →
