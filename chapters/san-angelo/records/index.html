Texas Public Information Act · Gov't Code ch. 552

Ask for the record. We'll put our name on it.

Texas law gives you access to almost everything San Angelo's government writes down. You should not have to identify yourself to your own city to exercise that right. Describe what you want; the Move Weight Foundation files the request as the requester and publishes whatever comes back.

What happens next

  1. We generate the legal request. A properly-cited request under Government Code ch. 552 on Foundation letterhead, as a PDF, with your identity absent.
  2. We deliver it by whatever method that body accepts. Some accept email; some require the vendor portal; SAPD requires mail or in person. The tool tells you which applies before you submit.
  3. The clock starts. Texas gives the body 10 business days to produce records, ask the Attorney General for permission to withhold, or tell us it needs more time.
  4. We publish what comes back. Documents, redactions, refusals, AG referrals, and cost estimates — all of it, in the public queue.

Straight about a legal detail

Under Gov't Code §552.234, an emailed request only starts the statutory clock if it goes to an address the body has designated for receiving requests. San Angelo routes requests through a vendor portal and has not clearly designated one. So for city records we treat the generated PDF and the portal as the real filing, and email as notice — not the other way round. We would rather tell you this than let you assume an email was legally served.

Costs

San Angelo charges $0.10 per page, $2 to certify an incident report, $6 for an accident report ($8 certified), and $7 for a clearance letter. Large requests can trigger a written cost estimate. The Foundation pays these from donations — you are never billed. Help cover them →

Know your rights under the Act

What is public. Almost everything. Emails, text messages about city business, contracts, invoices, calendars, memos, studies, bid documents, personnel policies, body camera policies, budget worksheets. The presumption in Texas law is disclosure.

What can be withheld. Narrow categories — certain law enforcement records, attorney-client communications, some personnel matters, competitive bidding information before award. Crucially, a body that wants to withhold must normally ask the Attorney General for permission within 10 business days. Silence is not a valid denial.

What you never have to do. Explain why you want it. State your purpose. Prove you are a resident. A governmental body may not require a reason as a condition of release.

What costs money. Copies, not access. If a body estimates over $40 in charges it must give you a written itemized estimate first.

Authoritative sources: Texas AG — How to Request Public Information · Overview of the Public Information Act · Public Information Act Handbook 2024 (PDF)

This is a plain-language summary of a statute, not legal advice. The Move Weight Foundation is not a law firm. For a dispute over a denial, the Texas Attorney General's Open Government Hotline is 1-877-673-6839.
