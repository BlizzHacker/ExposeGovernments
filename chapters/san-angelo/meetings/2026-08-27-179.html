STAFF REPORT 
Design & Historic Review Commission 
 
Analysis 
The applicant is requesting to restore the property by replacing rotted siding, repainting the exterior of the 
house, replacing the shingles on the front of the house, and replacing the roof. The applicant is also requesting 
to build a second story addition on the back of the house, extend the southwest corner of the guest house, 
move the driveway from the front of the house to the back alleyway, change garage door placement to face 
the new driveway, build a new fence towards the front of the house and side yard, move electrical wires 
underground, and add a back porch. All restoration and renovation efforts seek to maintain the property’s 
historical character and original aesthetic. 
 
The property was included in San Angelo’s Historic Resources Survey in 1984 as a contributing historic 
resource. The property was first listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1988 as the Henderson, S. 
L. House. According to the survey, it was constructed prior to 1910 and is an example of a Bungalow-
influenced home. It became listed on the register due to its architectural significance to the local community. 
 
According to the Engineers & Architects of America, the exterior of a bungalow-style house is typically defined 
by a large, covered front porch, with porch columns, as well as wood siding, shingles, or brick. The current 
request will not affect these key characteristics of the bungalow style, as almost all renovations will affect the 
backside of the property and won’t be immediately visible from the street. 
 
The applicant plans to paint the exterior of the house with colors from Sherwin Williams’ Historic Collection, 
which includes 2829 Classic White, 0050 Classic Light Buff, and 2841 Weathered Shingle. The existing siding 
will be reused where in good condition, or replaced with like-for-like material, and painted with the 2829 
Classic White. The roof will be redone with Standing Seam – Corten AZP Raw, and new shingles will be 1x6 
Select Tight Knot Western Red Cedar in in the Westwood, Sandhills color. The existing planter beds on the 
front of the house will be restored with the same stone as the current design. 
 
The applicant plans to expand the footprint of the house by adding a second story on the back side of the 
house (western side) which faces the alley. The addition will be ~950 square feet and will utilize the same 
exterior materials as the rest of the property, including re-use of existing windows for the windows facing 
west, and the new windows on the north and south facing sides will match the windows on the rest of the 
property as closely as possible. 
 
The applicant plans to expand the footprint of the guest house, on the southwestern side of the property, by 
about ~70 square feet. This will change the footprint of the guest house to a rectangular shape by filling the 
southwestern corner with a new bathroom. The addition will utilize the same exterior materials as the rest of 
the guest house, which match the rest of the property. 
 
The applicant plans to reconfigure the garage by moving the garage door from the east side to the south side 
for access to a new paved driveway that will connect through the alley. The new garage door will be in the 
same style as the current garage door and be painted 2829 Classical white. The existing driveway will be 
removed and replaced with new topsoil.  
 
Lastly, the applicant plans to replace fencing on the back and side of the property with T&G Savannah Fence 
with Gap Horizontal on top in a 1x6 Select Tight Knot Western Red Cedar. The color will be Westwood and 
Sandhills, which will match the shingles on the house, and will have a remote entry gate on the back side of 
the property as well.

Design & Historic Review Commission 
 
 
Certificate of Appropriateness:  
1. Every reasonable effort shall be made to adapt the property in a manner which requires minimal 
alteration of the building, structure, object, or site and its environment. 
The property is being adapted in a responsible manner which minimally alters the structure’s visible 
historic nature along street frontage while still refreshing the building with increased space and modern 
amenities such as buried electrical wiring. 
 
2. The distinguishing original qualities or character of a building, structure, object, or site and its 
environment shall not be destroyed. The removal or alteration of any historic material or distinctive 
architectural features should be avoided when possible. 
No distinguishing features are being destroyed or altered in a way that will diminish the character. The 
items being requested are additions and modifications that are on the back side of the property and will 
not affect key design characteristics which define the bungalow style that put it on the National Register 
of Historic Places. 
 
3. All buildings, structures, objects, and sites shall be recognized as products of their own time. 
Alterations that have no historical basis and which seek to create an earlier appearance shall be 
discouraged. 
These additions do not seek to unreasonably modernize the historical characteristics of existing 
characteristics. 
 
4. Changes which may have taken place in the course of time are evidence of the history and development 
of a building, structure, object, or site and its environment. These changes may have acquired 
significance in their own right, and this significance shall be recognized and respected. 
Any historical significance would not be threatened under the current scope of work. 
 
5. Distinctive stylistic features or examples of skilled craftsmanship which characterize a building, 
structure, object, or site shall be kept where possible. 
Major alteration to the stylistic features of the building are not within the scope of work for this request.  
 
6. Deteriorated architectural features shall be repaired rather than replaced, wherever possible. In the 
event replacement is necessary, the new material should reflect the material being replaced in 
composition, design, color, texture, and other visual qualities. Repair or replacement of missing 
architectural features should be based on accurate duplications of features, substantiated by 
historical, physical, or pictorial evidence rather than on conjectural designs or the availability of 
different architectural elements from other buildings or structures. 
Applicant plans to reuse and or repair as much as the existing structure where possible, and will only 
replace features with like for like materials where necessary. 
 
7. The surface cleaning of structures shall be undertaken with the gentlest means possible. Sandblasting 
and other cleaning methods that will damage the historic building materials should not be undertaken. 
Surface cleaning was not explicitly proposed as part of the application, so additional details may need to 
be provided on the planned cleaning methods. 

STAFF REPORT 
Design & Historic Review Commission 
 
8. Every reasonable effort shall be made to protect and preserve archaeological resources affected by, or 
adjacent to, any project. 
Staff are not aware of any such archeological resources. 
 
9. Contemporary design for alterations and additions to existing properties shall not be discouraged 
when such alterations and additions do not destroy significant historical, architectural, or cultural 
material, and such design is compatible with the size, scale, color, material, and character of the 
property, neighborhood, or environment. 
The proposed additions would not destroy material in an apparent way and the design is found to be 
compatible with both the surrounding neighborhood and the property itself. 
 
10. Wherever possible, new additions or alterations to buildings, structures, objects, or sites shall be done 
in such a manner that if such additions or alterations were to be removed in the future, the essential 
form and integrity of the building, structure, object, or site would be unimpaired. 
Additional details may be required to determine whether the addition in the rear could be removed 
while maintaining the historical integrity of the building. 
 
Recommendation: 
The staff’s recommendation is for the Design and Historic Review Commission to APPROVE the case as 
presented, subject to three (3) Conditions of Approval: 
 
1. The colors, dimensions, and materials of all improvements shall be consistent with the renderings as 
approved by the Design and Historic Review Commission. Minor deviations may be approved by the 
Planning and Development Services Director.  
 
2. The applicant must apply for and receive approval of all required permits through the Building Permits 
and Inspections Division. 
 
 
3. Given the size of the proposed addition, a replat of the property must be recorded before approval of 
the necessary building permits.
