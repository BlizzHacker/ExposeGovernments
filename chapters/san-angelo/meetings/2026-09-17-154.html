a. DD26-12: 226 S Abe -- A request for downtown design approval to paint the exterior of an existing 
building, replace windows and doors, add concrete for parking in the rear and a lamp post, and add 
lantern style lights to the front located at 226 S Abe. 
Austin Reed, Planning & Development Administrator, presented the case. 
Board Member David shared that the building was built in 1959 and said it was considered historic.  
Board Member Donna stated that a building is considered historic if it is of a certain age, contains 
architectural significance, and is in high quality condition. She felt that the elements of the building consider 
it historic. 

Aaron Vannoy said that this is not considered a historic building within the local ordinance. 
Board Member David wanted more clarification. 
Aaron Vannoy said that the state has guidelines of what a historic building is at the state level, but it is not 
considered as such at the local level. Aaron agreed that this is a high-quality Spanish style structure, but wants 
the decision to focus on what level of changes to the structure that the board would be comfortable with. 
Chair Chrys wanted to spend more time looking at the design plans and photos, to refresh the perspective of 
the rest of the board, since this case was reviewed at the prior DHRC meeting. 
Board Member David thought the changes were too intense of changes to the architecture. 
Board Member Brooks disagreed and stated that the changes would not affect the architectural quality or 
historic nature of the building. 
Board Member David had questions about the rear of the structure, Austin Reed requested those be deferred 
to the applicant. 
Chrys opened public comment. 
Jeff Padilla, the applicant, was available for questions. Jeff Padilla stated he had submitted side view 
renderings but they had not been present in the presentation.  
Board Member David asked if the building will have restrooms, Jeff Padilla said yes. 
Chair Chrys asked about the original roof tile colors, Jeff Padilla confirmed they were red and could go back 
to the original tile color if desired. 
Aaron Vannoy said it appears that at some point the clay tiles were painted. Jeff Padilla said it would not be 
difficult to repaint the clay tiles. 
Aaron Vannoy asked about the new color of the additions, Jeff said it will be black. 
Board Member Brooks spoke to the architecture of the original design and wanted clarification on the 
building’s function in respect to the openness. Jeff Padilla said it is an office space on the more enclosed side 
and it is very open on the garage door side. 
Board Member David wanted to ask what the building was planned to be, suggesting that if it was an event 
space that it would need handicapped spaces. Jeff Padilla said that the current focus is on the renovation of 
the building. 
Chair Chrys asked about not making the stucco changes, Jeff Padilla was open to that. 
Board Member David asked about paint colors, Austin Reed said it should have been in the staff report, Jeff 
Padilla said that the selected colors were off of the historic palette. 
Jeff Padilla recognized that Concho Ave is one of the entrances to downtown and this is an important building 
because of the traffic that it sees consistently. 
Chair Chrys likes the proposed work, but is concerned about modifying the stucco and harming the historic 
design aspects of it. 
Board Member Stacie likes the way it looks but is okay with not including stucco modifications.

in seeing a different roof color and maintaining the look around the front door

Board Member Brooks asked about buildings that are noncontributing non historic buildings and what design

Aaron Vannoy confirmed that they do not need to follow the historic guidelines and they just need to follow 
the downtown overlay standards. 
Board Member Brooks confirmed that a property owner has more development ability when it is not a historic 
building. 
Aaron Vannoy said that design decisions are made by the board and are always subject to appeal. 
Board Member David asked about if the building was instead on Chadbourne, or historic

Board Member Brooks confirmed that as part of a historic commission survey this building was not considered

Aaron Vannoy confirmed that this building is not considered historic, but if the board does not want to see

doorway

STAFF REPORT 
Design & Historic Review Commission – September 17th, 2026 
 
Analysis 
The applicant is requesting to restore and repaint the original structure and façade of the building, including 
the restoration and repainting of the damaged window casings, removal of the existing red tiling, installation 
of a 1920’s marquis-style awning, and the replacement of the existing projecting sign attachment with a time-
era metal replacement. Additionally, at the back of the property adjacent to an alley, the Applicant intends to 
remove an existing neglected carport for safety reasons, reinstall a short overhang in its place, pave a back 
parking lot, and install a mural on the inside of the metal fence to the west. Any restoration efforts shall 
prioritize the building’s safety while maintaining its historical character and original aesthetic.  
 
This property was designated as low-contributing Historic in the early 2000’s, and no historical survey provides 
documentation of previous conditions. Moreover, a faux metal wall covers half of the current façade. Surveys 
for the surrounding area (601-620 S Chadbourne) are shown to be constructed in the 1920’s in the Spanish 
style, with flat marquis-style awnings and large window fronts. The applicant intends to mirror this style with a 
speakeasy façade.  
 
The existing brick at this property has already been painted; the top section is a combination of cream and 
black, the middle is yellow, and the lower portion is unpainted brick which has been covered by red ceramic 
tiling, which is now falling off. The left side of the building has been covered with a metal wall to remediate 
the neglect and decay of the façade. Two projecting sign posts are attached to the top of the building. The 
Applicant intends to remove this metal wall and one of the sign attachments, revealing the façade below, and 
repaint the brick to a more uniform color from the historic palette (BEHR #BWC-25 Sandy Clay, shown below).   
 
The existing metal door will be replaced by two cedar doors with large windows, which are intended to fill the 
existing boarded casing. The current rotted wood will be replaced with the proposed wood framing consistent 
with the renderings shown below, in color BEHR #PPU24-23 Little Black Dress. Any brick removed during the 
addition of the wood framing will be used to repair damaged brick on the left-hand side of the façade, 
currently covered with the metal wall.  
 
The proposed marquis-style metal awning, painted to match the windows, will hang over the width of the 
windows. The Applicant intends to install recessed lighting underneath the awning, as well as hidden 
spotlights above to light the projecting sign in order to avoid an electric sign and preserve the historic 
character of the building.  
 
At the back of the lot, the Applicant intends to remove an existing metal carport due to damage, install a 
stucco wall in the color BEHR #PPU5-10 Heavy Cream, and replace the existing door with a secure door which 
meets fire code standards. The back of the lot will be paved according to the attached rendering to provide 
employee parking, and the Applicant intends to hire a muralist to paint a tribute to Latinx musicians on the 
back metal fence.  
 
The building located at 639 S Chadbourne is situated within the Historic Overlay Zone and classified as a low 
contributing building. While no surveys exist detailing the specific architectural style of this lot, the 
surrounding area is noted to be influenced by the Spanish Colonial Revival. The proposed work mirrors the 
structural components of 20’s era storefronts, making it a distinctive and aesthetically pleasing addition to the 
downtown area.

STAFF REPORT 
Design & Historic Review Commission – September 17th, 2026 
 
Analysis: 
The applicant is requesting to remove the Spanish tile on the canopy of the façade of the original structure 
and replace with a material that is custom made our of metal or aluminum. This applicant also recently 
replaced the roof of the structure with a like-for-like material and paint color, which was administratively 
approved by staff. While allowing the metal to replace the tile will match the roof, this will be visually different 
from the prior design of the clay Spanish tile. The owner has proceeded with the metal as they have prioritized 
the building’s safety, as the prior roof and façade were leaking and/or falling into disrepair, but has agreed to 
make any changes necessary depending on this board’s approval. 
 
The property is estimated to have been constructed in the early 1930s as a Gas Station, in response to the 
increased availability of oil from continuing discoveries in the Permian Basin throughout the 1920s and 1930s.  
 
The building located at 311 S Chadbourne is situated within the Historic Overlay Zone and was designated as a 
Medium Priority Contributing Resource in the Historic Resources Survey of Downtown San Angelo. The 
building was constructed in the Spanish Colonial Revival style, a distinct and aesthetically pleasing style that is 
important to the downtown area. 
 
Certificate of Appropriateness:  
1. Every reasonable effort shall be made to adapt the property in a manner which requires minimal 
alteration of the building, structure, object, or site and its environment. 
The property is being repaired in a responsible manner; however Staff believes allowing the removal of 
the Spanish tile would diminish the building’s architectural feature from the original period of the 1930s. 
  
2. The distinguishing original qualities or character of a building, structure, object, or site and its 
environment shall not be destroyed. The removal or alteration of any historic material or distinctive 
architectural features should be avoided when possible. 
The canopy is a public visual feature and allowing metal would noticeably change the building’s 
appearance and distinguishing feature. Replacing the Spanish tile with metal would introduce a material 
and style that is not compatible with the original design of the 1930s structure. 
 
3. All buildings, structures, objects, and sites shall be recognized as products of their own time. 
Alterations that have no historical basis and which seek to create an earlier appearance shall be 
discouraged. 
These additions seek to modernize the historical characteristics of the building. 
 
4. Changes which may have taken place in the course of time are evidence of the history and development 
of a building, structure, object, or site and its environment. These changes may have acquired 
significance in their own right, and this significance shall be recognized and respected. 
The applicant is seeking to restore the historically significant features on the front of the building. 
 
5. Distinctive stylistic features or examples of skilled craftsmanship which characterize a building, 
structure, object, or site shall be kept where possible. 
Major alteration to the stylistic features of the building is within the scope of work for this request.  
 

STAFF REPORT 
Design & Historic Review Commission – September 17th, 2026 
 
6. Deteriorated architectural features shall be repaired rather than replaced, wherever possible. In the 
event replacement is necessary, the new material should reflect the material being replaced in 
composition, design, color, texture, and other visual qualities. Repair or replacement of missing 
architectural features should be based on accurate duplications of features, substantiated by 
historical, physical, or pictorial evidence rather than on conjectural designs or the availability of 
different architectural elements from other buildings or structures. 
The applicant has demolished the Spanish tile canopy and replaced it with a new metal material that 
does not closely match the prior color or design. 
 
7. The surface cleaning of structures shall be undertaken with the gentlest means possible. Sandblasting 
and other cleaning methods that will damage the historic building materials should not be undertaken. 
Surface cleaning is not part of this proposal. 
 
8. Every reasonable effort shall be made to protect and preserve archaeological resources affected by, or 
adjacent to, any project. 
Staff are not aware of any such archeological resources. 
 
9. Contemporary design for alterations and additions to existing properties shall not be discouraged 
when such alterations and additions do not destroy significant historical, architectural, or cultural 
material, and such design is compatible with the size, scale, color, material, and character of the 
property, neighborhood, or environment. 
The removal and replacing of the Spanish tile with metal is not found to be compatible with both the 
surrounding neighborhood and the property itself. 
 
10. Wherever possible, new additions or alterations to buildings, structures, objects, or sites shall be done 
in such a manner that if such additions or alterations were to be removed in the future, the essential 
form and integrity of the building, structure, object, or site would be unimpaired. 
No new additions or alterations are part of this proposal. 
 
Recommendation: 
The staff’s recommendation is for the Design and Historic Review Commission to D E N Y the case and 
have the applicant replace the Spanish tile on the canopies.
