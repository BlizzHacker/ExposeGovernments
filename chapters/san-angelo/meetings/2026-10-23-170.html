STAFF REPORT 
Design & Historic Review Commission – October 23rd, 2026 
 
Background 
508 W Beauregard is a property located at the northwest corner of the intersection of W Beauregard Ave and 
S David St. The building on the site was originally constructed around 1959, and over the years it has 
functioned as a drive-in bank, which is the proposed use of the applicant. Though it was surveyed in 2006, it 
was not considered a contributing historic resource and does not appear on any historic registries. It sits 
within the River Corridor Overlay District. 
 
This property has been reviewed for River Corridor approval two times in the past.  
- 
In 2005, it received administrative approval to replace a canopy.  
- 
In September of this year, it received administrative approval to install new exterior doors and 
windows, which were deemed like-for-like. 
 
Other planning case history: 
- 
In 1996, it received approval for an 8’ variance from the 25’ required front yard setback to install an 
ATM on the site. The ATM proposed to be installed will be in a similar location to the one that was 
installed, and later removed, on the site because of the variance. The variance was also for 
maneuvering space related to the angled parking design utilized at the time. 
- 
In 1997, it received approval for a sign variance to allow an additional sign to be installed. The signs 
then installed because of this variance were the freestanding pylon sign and the monument sign. The 
sign ordinance has since been modified so that this sign variance is no longer relevant to this property. 
- 
In 2019, the property was rezoned from General/Heavy Commercial (CG/CH) to Central Business 
District (CBD) along with most property west of S Randolph St and east of Santa Fe Park Dr. 
Analysis 
The proposed exterior work on the property can be grouped into the following categories: 
 
Signage: 
The applicant is looking to install six (6) new signs for the property located at 508 W Beauregard. 
- 
One (1) freestanding sign on an existing pylon at the south side of the property, measuring 
approximately 70 square feet. 
- 
One (1) monument sign located at the corner of W Beauregard Ave and S David St, measuring 
approximately 49 square feet. 
- 
Two (2) attached signs, one on the east side measuring approximately 54 square feet and one on the 
west side measuring approximately 22 square feet. 
- 
Two (2) wayfinding signs, one located at the southwest entrance to the property, and one located at 
the northeast entrance to the property, each measuring approximately 11 square feet. 
All signs will use a white color in combination with the same three beige-brown colors that are represented in 
the exhibits, their CMYK values are the following: 
- 
Light Beige: C4.3 M29.4 Y45.1 K18 
- 
Medium Beige: C8.2 M48.2 Y71.4 K33.7 
- 
Light Brown: C15.7 M68.2 Y78 K64.3

review construction of any part of a structure visible from a public right-of-way. The proposed improvements 
shall be consistent with the respective design guidelines of the River Corridor Master Development Plan 
(RCMDP) for Commercial Use Signage.

Design & Historic Review Commission – October 23rd, 2026 
 
Analysis 
The applicant is looking to install two new portable buildings on the east side of 401 E Beauregard, that are 
intended to serve as additional office space for the San Angelo Police Department. The new office space will 
allow the department to relocate patrol operations from the basement and make room for more storage.  
 
The two buildings will be positioned parallel to one another with an approximately 10-foot-wide connecting 
deck/landing between them. ADA-accessible ramps will be incorporated into the deck design. The applicant 
has selected exterior finishes and colors to complement the existing Police Department facility and 
surrounding River Corridor. 
 
The new buildings will not be temporary structures, though they are also not intended to be long-term 
solutions for the applicant. The applicant plans to make use of the structures for, ideally, only the next 5 years. 
 
River Corridor Master Development Plan (RCMDP): Section 212 of the Zoning Ordinance requires the DHRC to 
review construction of any part of a structure visible from a public right-of-way. The proposed improvements 
shall be consistent with the respective design guidelines of the River Corridor Master Development Plan 
(RCMDP) for Commercial Use outside the Historic City Center, but in The River Front given its proximity to the 
Concho River. 
 
Site Design and Layout: 
The buildings are to be placed to the side/rear of the existing structure and match the current orientation of 
the existing buildings. They are also taking the place of an existing parking lot, which will help “eliminate 
uneconomical leftover areas.” The new structures will support the “360-degree” design treatment of the 
building, since the existing façade on the east side of the property is relatively minimally designed compared 
to the rest of the building. 
 
Architectural Detail: 
The removal of the existing parking lot supports the façade facing E Beauregard and helps eliminate empty 
space between the building and the river. However, the minimal design of the rear of the new building facing 
W Beauregard Ave generally be inconsistent with the established façade of the existing building. 
 
Building Materials and Color: 
The new buildings will use subtle colors that generally conform to the color scheme established by the design 
guidelines of the River Corridor. However, building materials and color generally differ from the existing 
building. The existing structure is constructed primarily out of concrete and brick, with the dominant colors 
generally being white, beige, and red, and the building putting lighter colors at the top and darker colors at the 
base. The proposed portable structures will be constructed out of lower quality materials with the dominant 
colors generally being shades of brown, with the roof being the darkest point on the structure. 
 
Roof Guidelines: 
The new buildings will contribute to the varied roofline of the existing building, which is one of the key design 
characteristics of the existing structure. The structure will add to the gradual “downward slope” of the roofline 
from east to west. However, the Roofing materials generally differ from the color and material of the 
existing structure and generally do not convey a sense of permanence and quality. The roof of the existing 
structure is flat, made of solid materials in concrete, and is lighter in color than the rest of the building. The 
proposed portable structure will have roofing materials made of lower quality, and the roof will be the darkest 
point on the building.

Lighting: 
The proposed lighting for the new buildings will support the entry features of the building, which is one of its 
distinctive architectural elements. 
 
Walls and Fences: 
The new buildings will have entryways, lighting, and windows that provide frequent offsets from large 
expanses of the wall on the internal sides. However, the side and rear of each building, which will be facing 
public right of way, have minimal design features to break up the large expanses of wall, and are generally 
inconsistent with the existing building. 
 
Gateway Opportunities and the River Front: 
The new buildings will be set back more than 75 feet from the Concho River, and the subtle and low-intensity 
color palette is complimentary to the surrounding natural landscape. 
 
 
Conclusion: 
While staff believe that the new portable structures would not match the established architecture of the 
existing building, staff are also aware that recommendation of higher quality building materials may cause an 
economic hardship. Staff believes it is still reasonable to recommend similar quality building colors & materials 
that more closely match the existing building.

Analysis 
The applicant is looking to install new signage on the north side of the property located at 38 N Chadbourne. The sign 
exceeds 50 square feet in area, which requires approval from the Design & Historic Review Commission. 
 
38 N Chadbourne was originally constructed around 1920, originally intended to be a restaurant. In the Historic 
Resources Surveys, it was first surveyed in 1984 and again in 2006 but was not considered a contributing historic 
resource in either survey. However, in the 2021 Historic Resources Survey, it was designated as a medium-priority 
contributing resource. 
 
The proposed sign is to replace an existing sign that was approved by the Design and Historic Review Commission in 
April 2017, with the case named RCC17-09. The sign that was approved was reviewed under the guidelines of the River 
Corridor Master Development Plan, as the property was not considered historic at that time. Now that the property is a 
contributing historic resource, it must obtain a Certificate of Appropriateness. 
 
Staff believe that the proposed signage supports the business located at 38 N Chadbourne and doesn’t detract from the 
façade of the structure for which the property has been deemed historic.
