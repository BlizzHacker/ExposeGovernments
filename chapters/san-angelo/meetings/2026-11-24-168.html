STAFF REPORT 
Design & Historic Review Commission – November 24th, 2026 
 
Analysis 
The Concho Connection, which is an area connecting S Oakes St to the riverwalk and is immediately to the 
west of the Oakes Street Bridge, is within the concept plan designed by the Parks & Recreation Department for 
the City Owned land surrounding the Concho River within the downtown. 
 
The 26-page Concho Connection Design packet focuses on the area described. The proposed work in this area 
includes new landscaping, paving, an entryway titled “CONCHO TRAIL TO RIVER,” trash cans, wayfinding signs, 
and new lighting. 
- 
The new entryway will be installed on the west side of S Oakes St, just south of 309 S Oakes St, 
alongside a newly paved sidewalk entrance. 
- 
The new lighting will come in the form of pedestrian pole lights, adjustable accent uplights, ground-
mounted lumaires, and bridge lumaires with columns. 
These new additions will require demolition of some paving and side rails but will not include removal of any 
trees or landscaping, or any structural aspects of the bridge. There are proposed art installations and murals, 
but their designs have not been finalized and are not included in this review. 
 
 
River Corridor Master Development Plan (RCMDP): Section 212 of the Zoning Ordinance requires the DHRC to 
review construction of any part of a structure visible from a public right-of-way. The proposed improvements 
shall be consistent with the respective design guidelines of the River Corridor Master Development Plan 
(RCMDP) for The River Front. 
- 
Gateway Opportunities and the River Front: New landscaping and plant life will help screen existing 
parking lots from the river. Proposed new plant life will be native plants. Proposed signage will be 
supported by additional landscaping. 
- 
Storm Water Detention: Proposed work will not require additional storm water detention. 
- 
Fences: No fences are proposed within project. 
Avoiding Critical Sites: Project area includes portions of the 500-year and 100-year floodway 
surrounding the Concho River, but proposed work will not affect the function of the floodway due to 
the lack of a significant increase in impervious surfaces and the expansion of native plant life and 
greenspaces which will help with water management. 
- 
Appropriate Location and Density: Proposed expansion of pedestrian facilities will help maximize the 
use of existing transportation infrastructure and support the river corridor which is a goal of the 
comprehensive plan. 
- 
Brownfield Redevelopment: Proposed work is not in a Brownfield area. 
- 
Erosion and Sedimentation Control: Proposed work will enhance vegetation and ground cover by 
native plant species, which will help mitigate sedimentation and erosion.  
- 
Storm Water Management: Proposed work will not increase the amount of impervious surface by 25%, 
which will therefore not require and increase in the rate and quantity of on-site water recharge. 
- 
Reducing Site Disturbance and Restoring Natural Features: Proposed work will minimize disturbances 
to the existing green spaces, and the goal of the project is to protect and enhance existing plant life. 
Existing natural features will be supported by native plants. 
- 
Reducing Site Water Use for Plant Materials: All proposed plants are either native to San Angelo or to 
nearby regions of Texas, which means they will have a reduced need for water since they are native to 
the climate.  
- 
Reduce Light Pollution: Proposed lighting will be targeted towards and focused on walking areas, and 
all light sources will be diffused to reduce glare and contrast. Light sources will also be DarkSky 
Approved. 

STAFF REPORT 
Design & Historic Review Commission – November 24th, 2026 
 
- 
Reducing Heat Island Effect: Newly paved walkways will be shaded with existing and future trees. A 
majority of the development area will maintain and enhance existing greenspace. 
- 
Encouraging Efficient Transportation Alternatives: Development is primarily focused on increasing 
access to pedestrian walkways, which encourages efficient transportation alternatives. 
 
Additionally, all proposed Signage will not use internal lighting. 
 
New Artwork. Included in the design packet are renderings of art installations. The Design and Historic Review 
Commission has the authority to review the location of art installations. The art installations are the following: 
- 
New murals at 309 S Oakes St and 315 S Oakes St. There are renderings of desert scenes with cowboys 
and cattle. Both properties were designated as medium priority contributing historic resources and are 
included in the Chadbourne Commercial Corridor historic district. Both properties were estimated to 
have been constructed between 1930 and 1935. Their original doors and windows have since been 
replaced and the original brick has since been repainted and covered with stucco. 
- 
A new statue along the new pedestrian walkway. The statue will likely be a metallic statue of a man on 
a horse. 
- 
New artwork underneath the Oakes Street Bridge. The bridge is listed on the National Register of 
Historic Places, but is not listed as a local landmark and does not reside within a Historic Overlay Zone. 
 
 
Recommendation: 
The staff’s recommendation is for the Design and Historic Review Commission to APPROVE the case as 
presented, subject to two (2) Conditions of Approval: 
 
Approval: 
 
1. The colors, dimensions, and materials of the proposed work shall be consistent with the renderings 
approved by the Design and Historic Review Commission. Minor deviations may be approved by the 
Planning and Development Services Director. 
2. The applicant shall obtain all necessary permits from the Permits and Inspections Division.

Analysis 
This is the current concept plan designed by the Parks & Recreation Department for the City Owned land 
surrounding the Concho River within the downtown. The project area begins at the park to the northeast of 
the Concho Ave bridge, and follows the Concho River upstream, ending at the park directly across the river 
from Central High School. 
 
The 9-page Visual Exhibits packet covers the entirety of the project area. It also contains general concept plans 
for Santa Fe Park: 
- 
Santa Fe Park, also labeled as “Master Plan – Zone A,” will have new paved pedestrian walkways, new 
artwork, new plazas, new lawns with shade, and new amenities. These amenities include a renovated 
golf course north of W Harris Ave, a Pro Shop Kiosk, a Prairie Habitat, a Berm Garden, a Restaurant and 
fountain, a Concho Habitat with Meadow, a Kayak Launch, a River Overlook and Bird Watching, a 
Fishing Deck, and Strolling Gardens with Space for Future Educational / Play Elements.  
 
The Parks and Recreation department is looking for a discussion on the concepts and for the Design and 
Historic Review Commission to make recommendations on the concepts. This concept plan is planned to be 
heard by City Council in December. 
 
 
River Corridor Master Development Plan (RCMDP): Section 212 of the Zoning Ordinance requires the DHRC to 
review construction of any part of a structure visible from a public right-of-way. The proposed improvements 
shall be consistent with the respective design guidelines of the River Corridor Master Development Plan 
(RCMDP) for The River Front. 
- 
Gateway Opportunities and the River Front: Any proposed buildings should be complimentary to their 
surroundings and be set back at least 75 feet from the riverbank. New landscaping and plant life should 
help screen existing parking lots from the river, utilize water-conserving plants and irrigation systems, 
and a unified tree plan should be selected to ensure the cohesive appearance of a block or a section of 
a corridor. Using walls or fences as a border to areas is not preferred, landscaping should serve as a 
buffer instead. Where walls are required, they should be designed with unique patterns, textural 
differences or offsets. 
- 
Storm Water Detention: If proposed work requires additional storm water detention, it should 
complement the surrounding landscape and should be located where they have the greatest overall 
benefit. Where possible, porous paving materials should be used in parking lots to allow for infiltration 
of storm water, thereby minimizing storm water runoff. 
- 
Fences: Chain-link fences should not be used, nor should barbed wire, razor wire, or similar wire 
fences. Fences should be constructed of predominantly natural materials, such as stone or wood, and 
should be accompanied by landscaping.  
Avoiding Critical Sites: Project area includes portions of the 500-year and 100-year floodway 
surrounding the Concho River. Proposed work should be careful not to negatively affect the function of 
the floodway, and applicant should avoid the expansion of impervious cover on land that is 5 feet 
above the elevation of the 100-year floodway. 
- 
Appropriate Location and Density: Proposed work should expand pedestrian facilities to help maximize 
the use of existing transportation infrastructure and support the river corridor, which is a goal of the 
comprehensive plan. 
- 
Brownfield Redevelopment: Proposed work is not in a Brownfield area. 
- 
Erosion and Sedimentation Control: Proposed work should enhance vegetation and ground cover of 
native plant species, which will help mitigate sedimentation and erosion. Proposed work should

protect hillsides using erosion control measures. 
- 
Storm Water Management: Proposed work should not increase the amount of impervious surface by 
25%. If proposed work does exceed this amount, it will require an increase in the rate and quantity of 
on-site water recharge. 
- 
Reducing Site Disturbance and Restoring Natural Features: Proposed work should minimize 
disturbances to the existing green spaces. A minimum of 75% of all species planted on the site should 
be from stock identified as native to the local area. In addition, a minimum of 75% of all trees and 
shrubs, by quantity, are to be native material. 
- 
Reducing Site Water Use for Plant Materials: Proposed work should use native plants that do not 
require maintenance irrigation after a 1- or 2-year period. In areas where the use of native plant 
materials has not reduced or eliminated the need for additional maintenance irrigation, use high 
efficiency irrigation technology and/or use captured rain or recycled water, to reduce potable water 
consumption for irrigation by 50% over conventional means. 
- 
Reduce Light Pollution: Proposed lighting should use broad color spectrums which appear closer to 
daylight color temperatures. It should also not exceed Illuminating Engineering Society of North 
America (IESNA) foot-candle level requirements as stated in the Recommended Practice Manual: 
Lighting for Exterior Environments. 
- 
Reducing Heat Island Effect: Proposed work on parking lots should minimize the heat island effect. This 
can be achieved by using high-albedo materials, using an open-grid pavement system, or by planting 
trees to use as shade cover. 
- 
Encouraging Efficient Transportation Alternatives: Proposed work should focus on increasing access to 
pedestrian walkways and bike infrastructure, which encourages efficient transportation alternatives. 
 
 
 
Action: 
Staff would like the Design and Historic Review Commission to review, discuss, and provide feedback to 
the Parks & Recreation Department on the concept plan. The final designs for the individual portions of the 
River Parks Master Plan will need to be reviewed by the Design and Historic Review Commission at a later date.
