The documents

Twelve records, published exactly as the City produced them. Nothing removed, nothing covered, nothing altered. Read them yourself — every claim on this site is anchored to one of these files, and you should be able to check our work.

Where these came from

These records exist because residents asked for them. The requests that produced this production were filed by the Safe and Sound Coalition, the Southaven grassroots group campaigning on the plant's air and noise permitting. The Move Weight Foundation is publishing and indexing them so they are searchable, citable, and permanent. More about the coalition →

The agreements

The three instruments that moved money, and the one that moved the tax bill.

Eff. 2026-01-06 Ad Valorem Tax Agreement — “Project Synergy” (MDA approved) 21 pp

The central document. Between DeSoto County, the City of Southaven, the County Tax Assessor and Tax Collector, and MZX Tech, LLC, with the Mississippi Development Authority joining by Certificate of Final Approval. Sets the fee-in-lieu at one third of taxes otherwise payable (§ 6) for up to ten assessment years (§ 4), binds future City and County boards (§ 3.b), and recites a capital investment of over $10 billion and “at least twenty (20) new jobs” (Recital B). Exhibit B governs valuation, depreciation and the annual fee calculation.

Water

Four productions of utility bills, plus the City's own explanation of how the meters work.

2026-05-22 Water Bills, production #4 27 pp

The largest production and the source of the consumption figures on this site. Contains bills for two different customers: MZX Tech at 2875 Stanton (accounts 00014274 and 00014275) and GXO Supply Chain Inc at 2400 Stateline Rd W (accounts 00012324 / 00012326). Read the customer name on each bill before quoting a number — the two are easy to confuse and most of the pages are GXO's.

2026-02-23 Email — the City's Billing Manager explains the water bills 2 pp

Publication policy

These documents are published whole. Nothing has been redacted, blurred, cropped, or edited. Every file is the City of Southaven's own production, byte for byte as it was released under the Mississippi Public Records Act. If a name, an address, a signature or a dollar figure appears in one of these files, it appears because the government put it in a public record and released it.

That is the whole point. A transparency project that redacts the records it publishes is asking you to trust its judgment instead of showing you the evidence. We would rather you check our arithmetic than take our word for it.

If you believe a document here contains something genuinely published in error by the City — not something merely inconvenient — tell us and we will look at it and say publicly what we decided.
