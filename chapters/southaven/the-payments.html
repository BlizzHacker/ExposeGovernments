Three agreements · December 2025 – February 2026

The Payments

In the ten weeks around the tax agreement, MZX Tech and the Musk Foundation put $6,690,900 in front of the City of Southaven. Here is each payment, what document created it, and when it was signed.

The sequence

Dates below are taken from the face of each document. We present them in order and without commentary about motive, because the order is the fact and motive is not something a contract can show.

  • 23 December 2025
    Memorandum of Understanding — $3,460,000 roads

    MZX Tech, LLC and the City agree on $3,460,000 for improvements to Stateline Road and Tulane Road — the roads serving the site.

    MOU for Stateline Rd, released by the City

  • 29 December 2025
    Musk Foundation Grant Agreement — $1,850,000

    A grant to the City from the Musk Foundation. The agreement contains a confidentiality provision at Section 6. Whether a Mississippi municipality can accept a grant conditioned on confidentiality, consistent with the Public Records Act, is a question the City's outside counsel would have had to consider.

    Identified in the City's own records index · requested in full

  • 6 January 2026
    Ad Valorem Tax Agreement takes effect 67% abated, 10 years

    The fee-in-lieu agreement's stated Effective Date. Two thirds of the project's property taxes are abated for up to ten assessment years, binding on future boards.

    The Board of Aldermen met that night. We pulled the City's published agenda for 6 January 2026 and read all eighteen items. Here is what it shows.

    The agreement, clause by clause · the agenda in full

  • 10 February 2026
    Donation Agreement — $1,380,900 to the Police Department

    MZX Tech LLC donates $1,380,900 to the City of Southaven for the exclusive use of the Southaven Police Department. Signed by Mayor Darren Musselwhite on 2–10–2026.

    SPD Donation Agreement, released by the City

  • 24 February 2026
    MDA Certificate of Final Approval executed

    The Mississippi Development Authority's Executive Director signs the certificate that makes the fee-in-lieu final under Miss. Code Ann. § 27-31-104(3).

    Exhibit A to the tax agreement · full application requested from MDA

What the agenda for 6 January 2026 shows

The Ad Valorem Tax Agreement states its Effective Date as 6 January 2026. The Mayor and Board of Aldermen met at 6:00 PM that evening. The City published the agenda. We host it in full; these four items are quoted from it verbatim.

6. Resolution for Emergency Expenditure for Traffic Signal at Intersection of Tulane Rd. and Stateline Rd.

13. Citizen’s Agenda: Jason Haley, Shannon Samsa

18. Executive Session: Economic Development (Potential Location of Business/Industry to City); Interdepartmental Personnel with No Action

Meeting of the Mayor and Board of Aldermen, Southaven, Mississippi · 6 January 2026, 6:00 PM · source PDF

Four observations, and one thing we cannot tell you

  1. No public item names the tax agreement. Across all eighteen items, nothing on the published agenda mentions MZX Tech, xAI, “Project Synergy,” ad valorem tax, or a fee-in-lieu — on the day the agreement took effect.
  2. Item 18 closed the meeting for economic development. The stated purpose is “Potential Location of Business/Industry to City.” Mississippi permits an executive session for that purpose, and closing one is not by itself improper. What it means here is that the public record of that evening stops at the door.
  3. Item 6 is Tulane and Stateline. Those are the two roads named in the $3,460,000 MOU with MZX Tech signed fourteen days earlier, now appearing as an emergency traffic-signal expenditure.
  4. Item 13 is two residents on the citizen’s agenda — Jason Haley and Shannon Samsa, the Vice Director and Director of the Safe and Sound Coalition. They put themselves on the record that night.
What we cannot tell you, and why

An agenda proves an item was scheduled. It does not show what was decided, how anyone voted, or what was said behind a closed door. The minutes for this meeting have not been published — the City's agenda portal carries agendas only.

So we are not saying the abatement was approved in secret. We are saying the public record of 6 January 2026 does not show it being approved in public, and the document that would settle it is a records request away. Mississippi also requires a certification of the reason for any executive session — that is a public record too. Both are in the template we have prepared →

The police donation, in its own words

1. Donation. The Donor agrees to donate the sum of $1,380,900 (the “Donation”) to the Recipient. Recipient agrees to use the Donation exclusively for The City of Southaven Police Department and in accordance with applicable Mississippi laws.

2. No Quid Pro Quo. This Donation is made without any expectation of goods, services, or benefits in return, other than any applicable tax deductions for the Donor under federal and state law, if any.

Donation Agreement, §§ 1–2 · source document

The donor is MZX Tech LLC, of 1450 Page Mill Road, Palo Alto, California. The recipient is the City. The agreement is governed by Mississippi law and states that it supersedes any prior agreements between the parties.

Two things on the face of the released copy

The donor never signed it. On the copy the City released, the DONOR signature block — signature, printed name, title and date — is blank. Only the City executed the document, on 10 February 2026.

§ 3 anticipates a vote that the released records do not show. The City “confirms that acceptance of the Donation complies with Mississippi municipal laws and has been (or will be) formally approved [e.g., city council resolution] to the extent required.” No minutes or resolution recording that approval has been released.

An unsigned counterpart is not by itself evidence of anything improper — parties often sign in counterparts and produce only one. We note it because the released record is the record, and this is what it shows. If a fully executed copy exists, producing it settles the point in a single email.
